# Stablecoin / Blockchain Payments in Underbanked Countries vs Mobile Money

Research date: 2026-09-23. Scope: 2023-2026 data; older data labelled. Figures taken from search snippets or secondary aggregators (not read from the primary document) are marked "(secondary)". Figures not verified at all are marked "unverified".

## 1. Adoption data (Chainalysis index, regional stablecoin share, country data)

### Takeaway
Crypto adoption ranks highest in large lower/middle-income countries (India, Pakistan, Vietnam, Brazil, Nigeria, Philippines). Stablecoins make up a large share of volume in Sub-Saharan Africa (about 43%, 2024 edition) and over half of fiat-to-crypto exchange purchases in Argentina, Brazil and Colombia (2025 edition). However, the Chainalysis indexes measure on-chain value received and exchange activity, not how many people make everyday payments. The value is dominated by large transfers.

### Cited Findings
**Chainalysis 2025 Global Crypto Adoption Index (published 2025-09-02; covers July 2024 - June 2025)**
- Top 20: 1 India, 2 United States, 3 Pakistan, 4 Vietnam, 5 Brazil, 6 Nigeria, 7 Indonesia, 8 Ukraine, 9 Philippines, 10 Russia, 11 UK, 12 Ethiopia, 13 Bangladesh, 14 Turkiye, 15 South Korea, 16 Yemen, 17 Thailand, 18 Venezuela, 19 Japan, 20 Argentina. Kenya and El Salvador are not in the top 20 — [Chainalysis, 2025-09-02](https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/2025-global-crypto-adoption-index/)
- Methodology: four sub-indices (centralized service value received, retail transfers under $10k, DeFi, and institutional transfers over $1M), weighted by PPP GDP per capita. The 2025 edition dropped the retail DeFi sub-index and added an institutional sub-index — [Chainalysis, 2025-09-02](https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/2025-global-crypto-adoption-index/)
- The 2024 edition (published Sept 2024) was not fetched in this research. Its country ranks are not recorded here. See Gaps.

**Sub-Saharan Africa (SSA)**
- 2024 edition: stablecoins were about 43% of SSA's total transaction volume. Nigeria received about 40% of all stablecoin inflows in the region. Nigerian stablecoin value in transfers under $1M approached about $3B in Q1 2024. Ethiopia had the fastest growth in retail-sized stablecoin transfers (+180% YoY) after the birr's roughly 30% devaluation in July 2024 — [Chainalysis SSA 2024, 2024-10-02](https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/subsaharan-africa-crypto-adoption-2024/)
- 2025 edition: SSA received more than $205B on-chain (July 2024 - June 2025), up 52% YoY. It is the third-fastest-growing region, and monthly volume peaked at about $25B in March 2025. Nigeria received $92.1B, nearly triple South Africa. Ethiopia, Kenya and Ghana complete the top five — [Chainalysis SSA 2025, 2025-09-10](https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/subsaharan-africa-crypto-adoption-2025/)
- 2025 edition, asset split of fiat purchases: in Nigeria, BTC was 89% of fiat purchases and USDT 7%. In South Africa, BTC was 74%. Retail transfers under $10k were 8% of value in SSA vs 6% globally — [Chainalysis SSA 2025, 2025-09-10](https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/subsaharan-africa-crypto-adoption-2025/) (as summarised by the fetch tool. The Nigeria BTC share looks unusual relative to the "stablecoin-led" narrative and should be verified against the full report.)
- Secondary coverage of the 2025 report also repeats the "43% stablecoin share" figure. That figure originates in the 2024 edition, so do not cite it as a 2025 number without checking — [tech.africa (secondary)](https://tech.africa/sub-saharan-africa-crypto-205bn/)

**Latin America (2025 edition, published 2025-10-02)**
- LATAM received nearly $1.5T in crypto from July 2022 to June 2025. Monthly volume peaked at $87.7B in Dec 2024 and was $47.9B in June 2025. Country totals: Brazil $318.8B (about one-third of the region), Argentina $93.9B, Mexico $71.2B, Venezuela $44.6B, Colombia $44.2B — [Chainalysis LATAM 2025, 2025-10-02](https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/latin-america-crypto-adoption-2025/)
- Stablecoins were more than half of all exchange purchases made with the Argentine peso, Brazilian real and Colombian peso (July 2024 - June 2025). 64% of LATAM activity happens on centralized exchanges — [Chainalysis LATAM 2025](https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/latin-america-crypto-adoption-2025/)
- The claim that "over 70% of Argentine exchange purchases are stablecoins" and "about 90% of Brazil trading volume is stablecoins" comes from aggregator snippets only. It is unverified against the primary source — [stablecoininsider (secondary)](https://stablecoininsider.org/stablecoins-in-latin-america-why-argentina-and-brazil-are-going-all-in-this-year/)

**Venezuela**
- USDT, called "Binance dollars" locally, is reported in use for groceries, condo fees, salaries and supplier payments, and receipts in Caracas are quoted in it. The country runs three reference rates: the official BCV rate (about 151.57 VES/USD), the parallel rate (about 231.76) and the Binance P2P USDT rate (about 219.62). Annual inflation was about 229% as of May 2025 — [Cointelegraph (secondary), 2025](https://cointelegraph.com/news/usdt-binance-dollars-replace-bolivar-in-venezuela)
- Ecoanalitica estimated at least 1.389B USDT traded on Binance P2P between June 11 and July 13 (2025), about 44M USDT/day. Reuters reportedly said the government allowed broader USDT use from June 2025, with banks selling oil-revenue USDT to businesses to pay suppliers — [Bitget News (secondary)](https://www.bitget.com/news/detail/12560605516560). The original Reuters article was not retrieved, so this is unverified at the primary level.

**Global user base (context)**
- a16z State of Crypto 2025 (published 2025-10-22): about 716M people own crypto, but only about 40-70M are active crypto users, with about 181M monthly active addresses. Stablecoin volume was $46T total and about $9T "adjusted/organic" over 12 months. Over 99% of stablecoins are USD-denominated. Crypto mobile wallet usage in Argentina rose 16x over three years, and wallet usage grew fastest in Argentina, Colombia, India and Nigeria — [a16z crypto, 2025-10-22](https://a16zcrypto.com/posts/article/state-of-crypto-report-2025/)

### Inferences
- The Chainalysis rankings mix retail and institutional activity. Starting in 2025 they explicitly reward institutional flows. A high rank for Nigeria or India is therefore not evidence of everyday retail payments.
- Compare a16z's 40-70M active users with mobile money's 593M monthly active accounts (see question 4). Mobile money has roughly 10x more regular users, although the populations overlap and are not directly comparable.

### Gaps
- Chainalysis 2024 index country ranks were not retrieved. From memory (unverified), India ranked 1, Nigeria 2 and Vietnam 5 in 2024.
- 2025 stablecoin share of volume for SSA, South Asia and Southeast Asia was not found in primary text.
- Country-level stablecoin data for Kenya, the Philippines, Turkey, Vietnam and India was not found in primary sources.
- The Chainalysis 2026 edition (expected around Sept 2026) was not checked.

## 2. Dominant use cases (savings/hedge, remittance, merchant POS, B2B, P2P)

### Takeaway
Measured "real" stablecoin payments are a tiny slice of on-chain volume. Within that slice, B2B dominates, followed by payroll/remittances. Card and merchant spending are the smallest categories. In high-inflation economies the main retail use is dollar savings and hedging, plus P2P FX. Merchant point-of-sale use is marginal except under hyperinflation (Venezuela).

### Cited Findings
- McKinsey x Artemis: of about $35T annualised stablecoin transaction volume, only about $390B (about 1%) is genuine end-user payments. The breakdown is B2B about $226B (about 58%, +733% YoY), payroll + remittances about $90B, capital-markets settlement about $8B, and card-linked spending about $4.5B. Real stablecoin payments are about 0.02% of global payments. The baseline is December 2025 activity annualised — [McKinsey (report page; fetch timed out, figures via secondary summaries)](https://www.mckinsey.com/industries/financial-services/our-insights/stablecoins-in-payments-what-the-raw-transaction-numbers-miss); [Artemis report PDF](https://reports.artemisanalytics.com/stablecoins/artemis-stablecoin-payments-from-the-ground-up-2025.pdf); [KuCoin news (secondary)](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/mckinsey-and-artemis-report-only-1-of-stablecoin-35t-volume-is-real-payments). The exact publication date was not confirmed (likely early 2026).
- Chainalysis names the main SSA uses as business payments, hedging against inflation, and smaller retail-sized transfers. It says stablecoin remittances cost "about 60% less" than traditional methods, citing a crypto-exchange executive (a vendor claim, not an audited price survey) — [Chainalysis SSA 2024, 2024-10-02](https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/subsaharan-africa-crypto-adoption-2024/)
- The Chainalysis 2025 SSA report mentions "energy and merchant payments" and cross-border trade flows but gives no quantified split by use case — [Chainalysis SSA 2025](https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/subsaharan-africa-crypto-adoption-2025/)
- Chainalysis LATAM 2025 lists the uses as inflation/FX hedge, cross-border remittances, and a "parallel financial system". No use-case percentages are given — [Chainalysis LATAM 2025](https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/latin-america-crypto-adoption-2025/)
- BIS: stablecoin cross-border flows were about $1.4T in 2024, with the largest share among EMDEs. Stablecoin flows appear largely unaffected by FX/capital-flow restrictions, unlike deposit dollarisation, which suggests they are used to get around capital controls — [BIS WP 1370 page](https://www.bis.org/publ/work1370.htm) (figures via search snippet, secondary. Publication 2026, exact date not confirmed.)
- Venezuela is the clearest case of stablecoins used at retail/merchant level (groceries, salaries), driven by hyperinflation and sanctions. See question 1 — [Cointelegraph (secondary)](https://cointelegraph.com/news/usdt-binance-dollars-replace-bolivar-in-venezuela)

### Inferences
- Card spending ($4.5B) is only about 1.2% of the $390B "real" payment volume. Merchant POS is therefore the least-proven stablecoin use case. B2B import payments and FX access are the proven ones.
- In Nigeria and Argentina the value proposition is "access to USD", not "a cheaper payment rail". Local payment rails (NIP in Nigeria, Pix in Brazil, UPI in India, M-Pesa in Kenya) already do domestic payments cheaply and instantly.

### Gaps
- There is no country-level split of retail stablecoin volume into savings vs payments vs remittances. No primary survey was found.
- The Castle Island / Visa "Stablecoin Payments" and Brevan Howard reports were not retrieved.

## 3. El Salvador Bitcoin legal tender outcome

### Takeaway
Bitcoin as legal tender failed as a payments and inclusion tool. Usage was minimal (1-2% of remittances, under 5% of sales). In January 2025 the law was amended under the IMF deal, which made acceptance voluntary, ended tax payments in BTC, and started the wind-down of the Chivo wallet.

### Cited Findings
- IMF Selected Issues (Country Report No. 25/68, published March 2025; analysis completed March 2, 2023, so the data is 2022-era) — [IMF CR 25/68 PDF](https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/cr/2025/english/1slvea2025003-print-pdf.pdf)
  - An NBER survey (Alvarez et al., 1,800 households, Feb 2022) found most users downloaded Chivo only to claim the $30 bonus, and 60% made no other transaction. About 40% of users were active six months after launch.
  - The FUSADES "Encuesta Dinamica Empresarial" (Oct 2022) found that 97.75% of businesses had never made a single sale in Bitcoin. The NBER survey estimates Bitcoin payments at 4.9% of sales, and about 80% of firms said they did not accept BTC.
  - A UCA public-opinion survey (Sept 2022) found that 66% consider Bitcoin a failed project and 77% oppose spending more public funds on it.
  - Central bank data showed only about 1.75% of remittances were sent via crypto wallets on average over the prior year.
  - Most Chivo transactions were free USD-BTC conversions. The IMF concluded that adopting Bitcoin as legal tender "has not led to progress in financial inclusion."
  - Context: remittances were 26% of GDP (2021). 97.25% of households have a mobile phone (2021 household survey). 68% of the population has a smartphone (2022 financial access survey).
- On January 29, 2025, the Legislative Assembly amended the Bitcoin Law as a condition of the $1.4B IMF Extended Fund Facility agreed in December 2024. Private-sector acceptance became voluntary, the obligation to accept BTC was repealed, BTC was removed as an option for tax payments, and Chivo is to be unwound. Sources disagree on wording: some report that bitcoin is "no longer legal tender", others that it keeps a voluntary legal-tender status and the word "currency" was removed — [Forbes, 2025-02-28](https://www.forbes.com/sites/digital-assets/2025/02/28/el-salvadors-bitcoin-law-changes-to-secure-imf-funding/); [Global Finance](https://gfmag.com/economics-policy-regulation/el-salvador-drops-bitcoin-legal-tender/); [IMF CR 25/68 elibrary](https://www.elibrary.imf.org/view/journals/002/2025/068/article-A001-en.xml)

### Inferences
- A government mandate, a $30 subsidy and zero fees together still did not create everyday use in a country that is already dollarised and has 68% smartphone penetration. This is strong evidence that the technology alone does not drive payment adoption.

### Gaps
- Status as of 2026 (Chivo fully shut down? government BTC purchases vs IMF review compliance) was not verified in this research round.

## 4. Mobile money comparison (GSMA, M-Pesa costs, Global Findex 2025)

### Takeaway
Mobile money is an order of magnitude larger in regular users than crypto and already reaches people who have a basic phone but no bank account. Findex 2025 shows that 900M unbanked adults have a phone, but only 530M of them have a smartphone. About 370M unbanked adults can therefore only be reached by feature-phone rails such as USSD or SMS.

### Cited Findings
- GSMA State of the Industry Report 2026 edition (press release 2026-03-24, covering 2025 data): 2.3B registered accounts (+268M, the largest annual increase ever). 593M 30-day active accounts (+15%), so about 25.7% of accounts are monthly active and about 75% inactive. Transaction value exceeded $2T in 2025 (it took 20 years to reach $1T and 4 years to double). Most new accounts came from Sub-Saharan Africa — [GSMA, 2026-03-24](https://www.gsma.com/newsroom/press-release/mobile-money-accounted-for-2-trillion-in-transactions-in-2025-doubling-since-2021-as-active-accounts-continue-to-grow/)
- GSMA SOTIR 2025 (press release 2025-04-08, covering 2024 data): 2B registered accounts, over 500M monthly active, 108B transactions, over $1.68T in value (+20% volume, +16% value). Sub-Saharan Africa is the most active region — [GSMA, 2025-04-08](https://www.gsma.com/newsroom/press-release/mobile-money-surpasses-two-billion-registered-accounts-and-over-half-a-billion-monthly-active-users-globally/)
- Sub-Saharan Africa mobile money value was about $1.4T in 2025 — [Connecting Africa (secondary), 2026](https://www.connectingafrica.com/mobile-money/-1-4t-flowed-through-mobile-money-in-sub-saharan-africa-in-2025-gsma)
- Global Findex 2025 (survey year 2024, 141 economies, 145k adults; released July 2025) — [World Bank Global Findex 2025](https://www.worldbank.org/en/publication/globalfindex); [BIIA summary (secondary)](https://www.biia.com/financial-inclusion-at-record-high-but-1-3-billion-still-unbanked-world-bank-global-findex-2025-report/)
  - 79% of adults globally have an account (74% in 2021). In low- and middle-income economies it is 75%, in Sub-Saharan Africa 58% (49% in 2021), in South Asia 80%, in MENA 53%, and in India 90%.
  - 1.3B adults remain unbanked. Over 650M of them live in eight economies: Bangladesh, China, Egypt, India, Indonesia, Mexico, Nigeria and Pakistan. 55% of the unbanked are women.
  - About 900M unbanked adults own a mobile phone, and 530M of them own a smartphone.
  - 15% of adults globally have a mobile money account. 84% of adults in LMICs own a mobile phone. 40% of adults in developing economies saved formally in 2024 (+16 points since 2021), and 35% in SSA (+12 points).
- The IMF (in its 2023 analysis) reported that in 2021, 69% of adults in Kenya and 60% in Ghana had a mobile money account, and more than 50% used it at least twice a month — [IMF CR 25/68](https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/cr/2025/english/1slvea2025003-print-pdf.pdf) (older data, 2021)
- M-Pesa (Kenya): P2P transfers to registered users are free for small amounts (a Ksh 1-100 band), rising to a maximum of Ksh 108. Sending Ksh 1,000 costs Ksh 13 (about 1.3%), including to Airtel Money and T-Kash since fees were harmonised across networks. No tariff change in 2025 — [Safaricom M-PESA charges](https://www.safaricom.co.ke/main-mpesa/m-pesa-for-you/tariffs-limits/consumer-tariffs-limits); [Techweez, 2024-12-30 (secondary)](https://techweez.com/2024/12/30/m-pesa-charges-withdrawal-and-send-money-fees-2025/). Cash-out (withdrawal) fees are separate and were not captured.

### Inferences
- For domestic P2P payments in Kenya-like markets, mobile money already offers free-to-about-1.3% fees, agent cash-in/out, and USSD reach. A stablecoin wallet adds USD denomination and cross-border reach, but not a cheaper domestic rail.
- Mobile money's own weakness is that about 75% of accounts are inactive in a given month. Registered-account counts overstate usage for mobile money as well.

### Gaps
- USSD vs app share of mobile money transactions was not found in the GSMA press releases. The full SOTIR report likely contains it.
- International remittance value via mobile money (2025) was not retrieved.
- GCash (Philippines) user numbers and fees were not retrieved.

## 5. Barriers to crypto payments

### Takeaway
The binding constraints are device and data access, fiat on/off-ramps (with bank bans in some countries), regulatory enforcement, and low active use. Volatility mainly hurts BTC-based models; stablecoins remove most of it but introduce issuer and depeg risk.

### Cited Findings
- **Devices and data:** in Sub-Saharan Africa smartphones are still less than half of connections, and SSA has the lowest smartphone adoption of any region. The mobile internet usage gap (people covered but not using mobile internet) is about 53% — [GSMA Mobile Economy SSA 2024 PDF](https://event-assets.gsma.com/pdf/GSMA_ME_SSA_2024_Web.pdf); [GSMA M4D blog](https://www.gsma.com/solutions-and-impact/connectivity-for-good/mobile-for-development/blog/the-state-of-mobile-internet-connectivity-in-sub-saharan-africa/) (figures via search snippet, secondary. The exact percentages were not read from the PDF.) The Mobile Economy Africa 2025 (Oct 2025) full data is behind registration — [GSMA Intelligence](https://www.gsmaintelligence.com/research/the-mobile-economy-africa-2025)
- **Devices among the unbanked:** Findex 2025 reports 900M unbanked phone owners but only 530M smartphone owners. Phone ownership is 75% among those with primary education or less vs 93% with secondary or more. Women are 9 points less likely to own a phone — [BIIA summary of Findex 2025 (secondary)](https://www.biia.com/financial-inclusion-at-record-high-but-1-3-billion-still-unbanked-world-bank-global-findex-2025-report/)
- **Financial literacy and usability (El Salvador, older data):** only 3.2% of adults could use a mobile money account without help. The IMF cites a limited ATM network for cash-to-Chivo conversion, technical glitches, and distrust as reasons for low use — [IMF CR 25/68](https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/cr/2025/english/1slvea2025003-print-pdf.pdf)
- **Regulation, Nigeria:**
  - The Central Bank of Nigeria's Feb 2021 directive barred banks from servicing crypto firms. On Dec 22, 2023 the CBN reversed it with "Guidelines on Operations of Bank Accounts for VASPs", but banks remain prohibited from holding, trading or transacting in crypto themselves — [Afriwise](https://www.afriwise.com/blog/cbn-lifts-ban-on-crypto-banking-transactions); [IBA overview](https://www.ibanet.org/Updated-overview-of-Nigerian-cryptocurrency-landscape)
  - Binance ended naira services on 2024-03-05 amid allegations of FX-rate manipulation, and two executives were detained — [The Africa Report](https://www.theafricareport.com/360383/nigerias-crypto-comeback-sec-approves-local-exchanges-post-binance-ban/)
  - The bank-backed naira stablecoin cNGN (a consortium including Access, Sterling, Providus and Interswitch) was launched afterwards — [Techpoint Africa](https://techpoint.africa/news/nigerian-banks-plans-develop-stablecoin/) (launch date and adoption not verified)
- **Regulation, capital controls:** BIS finds that stablecoin flows bypass FX and capital-flow restrictions, which raises the risk of regulatory crackdowns in EMDEs worried about "stablecoin dollarisation" — [BIS WP 1370](https://www.bis.org/publ/work1370.htm) (secondary snippet)
- **Low active use:** 40-70M active crypto users vs about 716M owners — [a16z, 2025-10-22](https://a16zcrypto.com/posts/article/state-of-crypto-report-2025/)
- **Merchant acceptance:** in El Salvador, even with a legal mandate, about 80% of firms did not accept BTC and BTC was 4.9% of sales (2022 data) — [IMF CR 25/68](https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/cr/2025/english/1slvea2025003-print-pdf.pdf)

### Inferences
- A product that requires a smartphone and data excludes roughly 370M unbanked phone owners (900M minus 530M, per Findex 2025) plus the 400M unbanked with no phone at all. This is an arithmetic inference from the Findex numbers.
- Off-ramping to local cash usually depends on P2P markets or exchanges. That dependence is the main exposure to bans (Nigeria 2021-2023, Binance 2024).

### Gaps
- Fraud and scam losses specific to these countries (e.g., Chainalysis Crypto Crime report data for Nigeria, Kenya or the Philippines) were not retrieved.
- Current (2026) status of Nigeria's regulatory regime (SEC licensing under the Investment and Securities Act 2025) is unverified.
- Kenya's VASP Act 2025 and the Philippines BSP VASP moratorium are unverified.

## 6. Remittance cost comparison

### Takeaway
The global average cost of sending $200 is about 6.4-6.5%, more than double the 3% SDG target, and Sub-Saharan Africa is the most expensive receiving region. Mobile money is the cheapest instrument in the World Bank data. Stablecoin transfers are cheap on-chain, but the end-to-end cost including on/off-ramp spreads has no World Bank-comparable measurement.

### Cited Findings
- Global average cost of sending $200: 6.4% in Q4 2023 — [RPW Issue (via Migration Data Portal / World Bank, secondary)](https://www.migrationdataportal.org/themes/remittances-overview). 6.49% in Q1 2025. Digital providers averaged about 3.65%, banks about 9.50%, and SSA is the most expensive region while South Asia is the cheapest at 4.80% — [Remitbee summary (secondary)](https://www.remitbee.com/blog/money-transfer/remittance/top-remittance-corridors-2025-fees-market-share)
- RPW Issue 54 (Q3 2025) exists, but its PDF returned HTTP 403 and was not read. A search snippet says mobile money remains the lowest-cost instrument for both sending and receiving remittances — [RPW Q3 2025 PDF](https://remittanceprices.worldbank.org/sites/default/files/2026-04/RPW_main_report_and_annex_Q325.pdf) (unverified at primary level)
- Chainalysis quotes an industry claim that stablecoin remittances are about 60% cheaper than traditional channels (Nigeria context) — [Chainalysis SSA 2024](https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/subsaharan-africa-crypto-adoption-2024/) (vendor claim, not measured by RPW)
- El Salvador: despite zero-fee Chivo transfers, only about 1.75% of remittances used crypto wallets (2022-era) — [IMF CR 25/68](https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/cr/2025/english/1slvea2025003-print-pdf.pdf)

### Inferences
- Low on-chain fees alone did not move remittance behaviour in El Salvador. The cost advantage matters most where FX controls create a large gap between official and parallel rates (Nigeria before 2024, Venezuela, Argentina). There the "saving" is mostly FX-rate arbitrage, not a lower fee.

### Gaps
- There is no independent, RPW-style dataset measuring all-in stablecoin remittance cost (on-ramp + network + off-ramp spread) per corridor.
- The Q3 2025 / Q1 2026 RPW global average was not confirmed from the primary PDF.

## 7. Evidence that devices, offline use and low-end phones matter

### Takeaway
Feature phones and USSD remain central in the markets with the most unbanked people. Mobile money was built for them, while crypto wallets mostly assume a smartphone and connectivity.

### Cited Findings
- Of the 1.3B unbanked, about 900M have a phone but only 530M have a smartphone (Findex 2025) — [World Bank Findex 2025](https://www.worldbank.org/en/publication/globalfindex); [BIIA (secondary)](https://www.biia.com/financial-inclusion-at-record-high-but-1-3-billion-still-unbanked-world-bank-global-findex-2025-report/)
- In SSA, smartphones are under half of connections and the mobile internet usage gap is about 53%. Almost 1B people in Africa do not use mobile internet (about 63% of the population) — [GSMA M4D blog (secondary snippet)](https://www.gsma.com/solutions-and-impact/connectivity-for-good/mobile-for-development/blog/the-state-of-mobile-internet-connectivity-in-sub-saharan-africa/); [TechAfrica News, 2026-03-05 (secondary)](https://techafricanews.com/2026/03/05/2025-vs-2026-mobile-industry-checkpoint-has-anything-actually-changed-for-africa/)
- Even in El Salvador (2021-2022), where 97% of households have a mobile phone, only 68% of people had a smartphone — [IMF CR 25/68](https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/cr/2025/english/1slvea2025003-print-pdf.pdf)
- Phone ownership has a gender gap of 9 points (Findex 2025) — [BIIA (secondary)](https://www.biia.com/financial-inclusion-at-record-high-but-1-3-billion-still-unbanked-world-bank-global-findex-2025-report/)

### Inferences
- A payment device or wallet aimed at the unbanked has to work without a smartphone and with intermittent connectivity (USSD/SMS, NFC card, offline signing) to reach the 370M+ unbanked who own only basic phones. It also has to integrate with cash agents and mobile money for on/off-ramps rather than compete with them.

### Gaps
- No data was found on shared-phone prevalence (multiple users per handset) in 2023-2026 sources.
- No data was found on the USSD share of mobile money transactions.
- No real-world adoption data was found for offline or feature-phone crypto wallets (e.g., USSD-based stablecoin services in Africa).
